01 · Abstract

Abstract

This project asks whether search-performance signals can be used to prioritize content pages for human review when deciding which pages may have an observed CTR opportunity. The workflow uses March 2026 content-performance records and decision-time signals including Google Search Console impressions, clicks, CTR, and average position. A Random Forest was compared with a transparent Week-5 baseline and then evaluated under a stricter grouped-by-client validation design. On the Week-5 time-aware validation, the measured Precision@50 was 1.000 for the Random Forest compared with 0.860 for the baseline, while the grouped-by-client validation measured 1.000 for both. These results support using the output as directional decision-support for human review, not as evidence that changing a page will cause higher clicks or improved search performance.

02 · Introduction

Introduction / Problem Statement

Content and SEO teams may have many pages that could potentially be reviewed, but manual prioritization can be difficult when the available portfolio is large. This project studies a narrower decision-support question:

Research question: Can observed search-performance signals be used to rank pages so that a human reviewer can inspect potentially valuable CTR opportunities first?

The goal is therefore not to automatically rewrite pages or predict guaranteed future performance. Instead, the model produces a ranked review queue. The queue is intended to help a content or SEO team decide what to inspect first.

04 · Methodology

Methodology

Target definition

The modeling target is a binary future-performance outcome derived from the available content-performance data. A positive target indicates that the observed future click outcome met the modeling definition used in the Week-5 workflow.

Baseline

The baseline is a transparent ranking rule based on search visibility and CTR-related signals. Its purpose is to provide a simple reference against which the machine-learning ranking can be compared.

Model

The machine-learning model is a Random Forest trained using the decision-time search-performance features. Training was limited to 300,000 rows for practical execution time.

Validation design

The Week-5 workflow used a time-aware split. The Week-6 audit added a grouped-by-client split so that clients in the validation set were separated from the clients used for training.

Why the second split matters: A model can appear strong when related observations from the same client occur across training and validation. Grouping by client tests whether the ranking remains useful when the validation clients are not represented in the training data.

Leakage audit

Features were reviewed for whether they would be available at the decision point. The workflow focuses on search-performance signals available before the future target period. Future click outcomes are used to define the target rather than as model input.

The validation audit also checked whether the evaluation design could make the model appear stronger than it would under a stricter split. Results are therefore interpreted as measured validation results, rather than causal evidence.

06 · Limitations

Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The model is a prioritization tool, not a causal model of what will happen after changing a page.
  • Precision@50 is measured on the available validation data and does not establish production performance.
  • The grouped validation produced the same measured Precision@50 for the baseline and Random Forest, so the evidence does not show a clear machine-learning advantage under that stricter design.
  • The model does not understand page intent, content quality, business priorities, brand constraints, seasonality, or the suitability of a proposed editorial change.
  • Training used a 300,000-row sample for practical execution rather than the complete modeling population.
  • The results are directional and should not be assumed to transfer unchanged to different clients, time periods, or content populations.
Claim boundary: The strongest supported claim is that the workflow produced a ranked decision-support queue and achieved the reported validation metrics. It does not establish that acting on the recommendations will cause higher clicks, higher rankings, or changes in search-engine behaviour.
07 · Recommendations

Ranked Recommendations

The model output is converted into a practical review queue. The action is to inspect a page, not automatically change it.

01

REVIEW_CTR

Reason code: CTR_OPPORTUNITY

Prioritize pages with meaningful search visibility and relatively low observed CTR for human review.

02

HUMAN_REVIEW

Reason code: REVIEW_BEFORE_CHANGE

Review the page context before proposing any content or metadata change.

Human review rules

  • Check whether the page's search intent matches the query demand.
  • Check whether the title and snippet accurately represent the page.
  • Check whether the page is already well optimized.
  • Check business, brand, and editorial constraints.
  • Check seasonality or unusual demand before changing content.
  • Record the reason for any change independently of the model score.

What should NOT be automated

  • Automatic title or metadata rewriting.
  • Automatic deletion or consolidation of pages.
  • Automatic changes to canonicalization or indexing directives.
  • Automatic publishing of content changes.
  • Automatic claims that a page will gain clicks or rankings.

The queue is intended to answer "what should a human review first?" rather than "what change should the system make automatically?"
